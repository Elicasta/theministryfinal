# THE IMPORTANCE OF INQUIRING

**Bible Study | 30–45 Minutes**

**Main Scriptures:** 1 Samuel 30:6–8; Joshua 9:14–16 (KJV)

**Central Message:** Wisdom begins when we recognize what we do not know, seek God, receive counsel, and obey what is right.

**Supporting Texts:** Proverbs 3:5–7; Proverbs 12:15; Proverbs 15:22; 1 Samuel 30:1–20; 1 Samuel 23:1–5; James 1:5,22; Acts 13:22; Joshua 9:3–27.

## THE IMPORTANCE OF INQUIRING

This morning, I want to talk to you about the importance of inquiring.

Many times we fail to receive counsel, not because guidance or leadership is absent, but because we do not ask. How many times have we made a decision without inquiring of the Lord?

This lesson contrasts David's response at Ziklag with Israel's response to the Gibeonites.

Central message: Recognize what you do not know. Seek God's wisdom. Receive counsel. Obey what is right.

---

## HAVE YOU INQUIRED?

When was the last time you genuinely stopped and asked: Lord, what should I do?

Many times, the problem is not that counsel was unavailable. It is that we never asked.

Announcing what we plan to do is not the same thing as asking for guidance.

---

## WHAT DOES IT MEAN TO INQUIRE?

To inquire means to ask for information, understanding, or direction.

Inquiring begins with the acknowledgment that I do not know enough to decide wisely. It is not a speech about what I have already decided.

An inquiry leaves room for information that may change my mind. It is asking with the intention to receive something I did not have.

---

## WE DON'T ALWAYS KNOW WHAT WE THINK WE KNOW

Why is inquiry important? Our own understanding is limited.

Sometimes we know one part of a story but assume we know everything. Experience is useful, but it does not make us all-knowing.

Wisdom starts when we admit that there is information we may not possess.

## WHY DON'T WE INQUIRE?

We assume we already know the answer.

We do not want to hear an answer that contradicts our desires.

We are afraid to look inexperienced or uninformed.

We have already made up our minds and want approval, not counsel.

Ask yourself: Would I still ask for advice if I knew it might challenge what I want?

## DAVID INQUIRED OF THE LORD

In 1 Samuel 30, David and his men returned to Ziklag and found their city burned.

The Amalekites had taken their wives and children captive. The men wept until they had no strength to weep. Then the men became so distressed they spoke of stoning David.

David had lost his home, his family was missing, and his own life was under pressure.

Pressure did not change where David sought direction.

## PRESSURE DIDN'T CHANGE DAVID'S RESPONSE

David knew his wives and children were at stake. He also knew his own life was in danger.

He could have reacted immediately, but he stopped and asked the Lord.

Sometimes we mistake urgency for direction. A real need does not mean we should abandon wisdom.

Ask: When did you last stop and inquire in the middle of a difficult situation?

## DAVID DIDN'T JUST ASK. HE OBEYED.

God directed David to pursue, and David obeyed.

Six hundred men began the pursuit. Two hundred remained at the brook Besor because of exhaustion. David continued with four hundred.

They found an abandoned Egyptian servant who led them to the Amalekites. David fought and recovered the captives and possessions.

## INQUIRING WAS DAVID'S HABIT

Ziklag was not the first time David inquired.

In 1 Samuel 23, David heard that the Philistines were attacking Keilah. He inquired before acting. When his men expressed fear, he inquired again, and God reaffirmed His direction.

Inquiring was part of how David approached decisions, not merely a desperate last resort.

## THE COVENANT MADE WITHOUT INQUIRY

Joshua 9 gives the opposite example.

The people of Gibeon heard what Israel had done to Jericho and Ai. Afraid for their lives, they decided to deceive the Israelites.

They put on old garments, carried worn shoes, patched wine containers, and brought dry, moldy bread to make it look as though they came from far away.

They then asked Joshua and Israel to make a covenant.

## THE EVIDENCE LOOKED CONVINCING

The Israelites examined the provisions. Everything seemed to support the Gibeonites' explanation.

But appearance was not the full truth.

Many times we assume we know, because the evidence looks convincing. Yet we are working from incomplete information.

They had an opportunity to ask God, but did not.

## WHAT HAPPENS WHEN WE DON'T INQUIRE?

We can misunderstand motives, enter commitments too quickly, act on incomplete information, or create problems that affect other people.

How often do we react without hearing the whole story? How often do we decide under pressure without asking what God wants?

Some mistakes are hard to reverse. Wise inquiry can prevent avoidable consequences.

---

## IT TAKES HUMILITY TO INQUIRE

We must recognize that we are not all-knowing.

Inquiring requires humility. We must be willing to say “I don't know,” “I might be wrong,” or “I need help.”

Wise believers do not confuse independence with maturity.

---

## HOW DO WE LEARN TO INQUIRE?

First: stop and recognize the need. What do I know? What am I missing? Am I acting from pressure or emotion?

Second: pray to the Lord. Ask, “Lord, what should I do?”

Third: stop and listen. Examine your motives and any impressions against Scripture. God's direction will not contradict His Word. Not every feeling is divine instruction.

Fourth: seek godly counsel. A pastor or spiritually mature believer may have experience you do not.

Fifth: obey what is right. Ask with a willingness to receive and act on truth.

## TWO STORIES. TWO RESPONSES.

David faced devastating pressure and sought God. Joshua's leaders saw convincing evidence but failed to ask the Lord.

One story illustrates the wisdom of inquiring. The other warns us about acting without sufficient guidance.

God may not disclose every fact about tomorrow, but He calls us to seek wisdom and trust Him.

## BEFORE YOU REACT, INQUIRE.

When we fail to inquire, we risk acting without the information we need. Without understanding, we can make mistakes.

When we inquire, we seek wisdom through prayer, Scripture, and godly counsel.

Before you make the decision, inquire. Before you react, inquire. Before you commit, inquire. And when you receive godly direction, be willing to obey.

Closing prayer: Lord, teach us to trust You. Give us humble hearts willing to receive counsel and correction. Help us walk in Your Word rather than rely only on our own understanding. In Jesus' name, amen.
