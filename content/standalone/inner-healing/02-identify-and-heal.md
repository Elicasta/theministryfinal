:::cue section | PART THREE | Identifying Hurt
# Identifying Hurt

:::cue verse | THE DANGER OF SUPERFICIAL PEACE | Jeremiah 6:14 (KJV)
Jeremiah 6:14 (KJV) They have healed also the hurt of the daughter of my people slightly, saying, Peace, peace; when there is no peace.

I find this scripture interesting because God was addressing people who were treating a serious problem as though it were something small.

They were declaring peace when there was no peace.

And sometimes we do the same thing internally. We convince ourselves that we’re okay. We say we’ve moved on, that nothing is wrong, and that everything is fine.

:::cue statement | A NECESSARY DISTINCTION | Living with something is not the same as being healed from it.
But just because we have learned to live with something doesn’t mean that it has been healed.

So how do we identify what is actually hurting us?

:::cue verse | DRAWING OUT THE DEEP WATER | Proverbs 20:5 (KJV)
Proverbs 20:5 (KJV) Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water; but a man of understanding will draw it out.

:::cue steps | FIVE QUESTIONS | Who? 
## 1. Who Hurt You?

Put a name on it.

Who was actually responsible for the pain you experienced?

Was it a parent? A friend? A spouse? A leader? Someone you trusted?

Sometimes we are unwilling to acknowledge who actually hurt us, and because of that, we begin projecting that pain onto everyone else.

When you identify who actually hurt you, you’ll stop blaming people who are not responsible for your current pain.

You’ll begin to recognize that not everyone is the person who hurt you.

:::cue steps | FIVE QUESTIONS | Who?;What?
## 2. What Did They Do?

What actually happened to you?

Not just how you felt about it, but what actually took place?

What was said? What was done? What happened?

:::cue verse | FACTS BEFORE ASSUMPTIONS | Proverbs 18:13 (KJV)
Proverbs 18:13 (KJV) He that answereth a matter before he heareth it, it is folly and shame unto him.

Sometimes we need to be able to separate the facts of what happened from the emotions that came afterward.

Because when we don’t understand what actually hurt us, we begin treating every similar situation as a trigger.

But when you recognize what actually happened, you’ll stop treating every situation as though it is the same situation that hurt you.

:::cue statement | DISCERNING THE PRESENT | Not every disagreement is rejection. Not every correction is an attack.
Not every disagreement is rejection. Not every correction is an attack. Not every difficult conversation is someone trying to hurt you.

:::cue steps | FIVE QUESTIONS | Who?;What?;How?
## 3. How Were You Hurt?

Were you hurt physically, emotionally, or mentally?

Was it something someone did to you? Something someone said? Was it a betrayal, abandonment, rejection, or disappointment?

:::cue verse | THE WEIGHT OF OFFENSE | Proverbs 18:19 (KJV)
Proverbs 18:19 (KJV) A brother offended is harder to be won than a strong city: and their contentions are like the bars of a castle.

It’s important to understand how we were hurt because not every wound is the same.

And when we fail to distinguish between different kinds of pain, we begin treating every offense as though it deserves the same response.

But when you understand how you were hurt, you’ll stop giving every offense the same amount of importance and weight.

You will begin learning to respond to situations according to what they actually are, rather than according to how much they remind you of something else.

:::cue steps | FIVE QUESTIONS | Who?;What?;How?;Why?
## 4. Why Were You Hurt?

What was the actual reason you were hurt?

This is an important question because sometimes we spend years blaming ourselves for things that were never our fault.

We ask ourselves, “What did I do wrong? Why wasn’t I good enough? Why did they treat me that way?”

But sometimes the answer is much simpler than we want to believe.

People can be cruel. People can be selfish. People can be evil.

Not everything someone does to you is a reflection of something wrong with you.

Sometimes people make terrible decisions, and you suffer the consequences of those decisions.

When you begin recognizing that, you’ll stop making yourself the reason for all your problems.

You’ll understand that what someone did to you doesn’t have to define who you are.

:::cue steps | FIVE QUESTIONS | Who?;What?;How?;Why?;When?
## 5. When Were You Hurt?

This one can be difficult.

Because many times we were hurt as children or young adults, and we’ve buried those experiences so deeply that we’ve almost forgotten about them.

But although we may have forgotten about the situation, the effects of that situation can still influence our lives.

Sometimes we develop ways of thinking and behaving when we’re young because of what we experienced, and we carry those same patterns into adulthood.

We learned to protect ourselves, to avoid people, to expect rejection, or to keep our emotions hidden.

:::cue verse | WHEN FAMILY LETS US DOWN | Psalm 27:10 (KJV)
Psalm 27:10 (KJV) When my father and my mother forsake me, then the LORD will take me up.

And now, years later, those same patterns continue affecting our relationships, our decisions, and the way we see ourselves.

Something that happened twenty years ago can still be influencing how you respond to something that happened yesterday.

But when you begin recognizing when the wound was formed, you’ll stop bringing your past into your present.

You begin realizing that you are not the same person you were when that happened.

You are no longer in that situation.

And the people who are in your life today should not have to pay for what happened to you years ago.

:::cue section | PART FOUR | How Do We Heal?
# How Do We Heal?

:::cue verse | THE HEART NEEDS ATTENTION | Proverbs 17:22 (KJV)
Proverbs 17:22 (KJV) A merry heart doeth good like a medicine: but a broken spirit drieth the bones.

:::cue verse | KEEP YOUR HEART | Proverbs 4:23 (KJV)
Proverbs 4:23 (KJV) Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life.

Now, I want to pause here because the answer to this question is almost always the same.

:::cue statement | AN ESSENTIAL FIRST STEP | Forgiveness.
Forgiveness.

It seems like every message we preach about healing eventually ends with forgiveness.

And forgiveness is absolutely necessary.

But today, I want to take this a little further than forgiveness.

Because I think sometimes we teach people to forgive without ever teaching them what it actually means to heal.

:::cue section | BIBLICAL EXAMPLE | David and Saul: Choosing Not to Retaliate
# David and Saul: Choosing Not to Retaliate

:::cue verse | DAVID REFUSES RETALIATION | 1 Samuel 24:12 (KJV)
1 Samuel 24:12 (KJV) The LORD judge between me and thee, and the LORD avenge me of thee: but mine hand shall not be upon thee.

Look at the relationship between David and Saul.

Saul had turned against David. He pursued him, wanted to kill him, and made his life incredibly difficult.

David had every reason to resent Saul.

And yet, when David had an opportunity to kill Saul, he refused.

He had the ability to retaliate. He could have taken matters into his own hands and justified it by pointing to everything Saul had done to him.

But he didn’t.

:::cue verse | VENGEANCE BELONGS TO GOD | Romans 12:19 (KJV)
Romans 12:19 (KJV) Dearly beloved, avenge not yourselves, but rather give place unto wrath: for it is written, Vengeance is mine; I will repay, saith the Lord.

David was able to maintain his composure even while dealing with someone who had treated him unjustly.

He decided that Saul’s actions were not going to determine his own actions.

He would not allow the way Saul treated him to determine the kind of man he would become.

But notice something else.

:::cue verse | FORGIVENESS AND DISTANCE | 1 Samuel 24:22 (KJV)
1 Samuel 24:22 (KJV) And David sware unto Saul. And Saul went home; but David and his men gat them up unto the hold.

David spared Saul, but he didn’t immediately go back to living under Saul’s authority as though nothing had happened.

He continued maintaining distance.

And I think that’s important because sometimes we confuse forgiveness with the immediate restoration of a relationship.

David could choose not to retaliate while still recognizing the danger of the situation.

Which brings me to these questions:

:::cue steps | THREE QUESTIONS | What is forgiveness?;How do we know we have healed?;How do we forgive when it is difficult?
What is forgiveness? How do we actually know we have healed? And how do we forgive when it’s difficult?

:::cue section | THE DISTINCTION | Forgiveness Does Not Equal Healing
# Forgiveness Does Not Equal Healing

:::cue verse | FORGIVE AS GOD FORGAVE | Ephesians 4:31–32 (KJV)
Ephesians 4:31–32 (KJV) Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ’s sake hath forgiven you.

:::cue contrast | TWO DIFFERENT WORKS | Forgiveness releases the debt;Healing restores what was wounded
* Forgiveness does not equal healing. Forgiveness is necessary, but forgiveness and healing are not exactly the same thing. Forgiveness deals with what we are choosing to release. Healing deals with what has happened within us.

* Many times we think, “Well, I forgave this person, so I must be okay now.” And then we’re surprised when we still feel pain, when certain memories still bother us, or when certain situations still affect us. We begin questioning whether we’ve actually forgiven them.

* But we make a mistake anytime we confuse letting go of the debt with actually feeling compensated or whole. Forgiveness means that I am choosing not to continue holding this debt against you. But that doesn’t automatically mean that everything you damaged has been restored.

* Healing is separate from forgiveness. I can forgive you and still not forget what you did. The memory of what happened can remain even after I’ve made the decision to forgive.

:::cue verse | KEEP FORGIVING | Colossians 3:13 (KJV)
Colossians 3:13 (KJV) Forbearing one another, and forgiving one another, if any man have a quarrel against any: even as Christ forgave you, so also do ye.

:::cue contrast | THE BROKEN LEG ILLUSTRATION | I can forgive the person;My leg still needs healing
* Your actions can still cause me pain. Think about it this way: If you accidentally break my leg, I can forgive you for breaking my leg. I can genuinely forgive you. I don’t have to be angry with you, and I don’t have to hold it against you. But guess what? My leg is still broken. The pain is still there. I forgave you, but the injury continues to hurt until it heals. Forgiveness didn’t automatically restore the bone. Healing is still necessary.

* Healing starts with forgiveness, but that’s not how the entire process is resolved. There is more that has to happen. There is something within us that needs to be restored.

And I think sometimes that’s where we struggle.

We’ve made the decision to forgive, but we haven’t allowed ourselves to go through the process of healing.

We’re frustrated because we think we should be over it by now.

But just because you’ve forgiven someone doesn’t mean that the pain they caused has immediately disappeared.

:::cue section | PART FIVE | Healing Can Be Progressive
# Healing Can Be Progressive

:::cue verse | MARK 8:22–25 | Mark 8:22–25 (KJV)
Mark 8:22–25 (KJV)

In this passage, Jesus encounters a blind man in Bethsaida.

The people bring this man to Jesus and ask Him to touch him.

Jesus takes the man by the hand, leads him out of the town, and begins healing him.

But something very interesting happens.

After Jesus first touches the man, He asks him whether he can see anything.

The man responds in verse 24:

:::cue verse | THE FIRST TOUCH | Mark 8:24 (KJV)
Mark 8:24 (KJV) And he looked up, and said, I see men as trees, walking.

:::cue progression | A HEALING IN STAGES | Blindness;Partial sight
The man could see, but he couldn’t see clearly.

Then the Bible tells us in verse 25:

:::cue verse | THE SECOND TOUCH | Mark 8:25 (KJV)
Mark 8:25 (KJV) After that he put his hands again upon his eyes, and made him look up: and he was restored, and saw every man clearly.

:::cue progression | A HEALING IN STAGES | Blindness;Partial sight;Clear sight
Jesus touched him again, and his sight was completely restored.

Now, I’m not saying that God has to heal everyone in stages.

But what I find interesting about this miracle is that we see a man who experienced healing before he experienced complete clarity.

He was no longer in the same condition he was in before Jesus touched him, but there was still more restoration to take place.

And sometimes that’s what healing looks like in our own lives.

We have made progress. We’re not where we used to be. Certain things no longer affect us the same way.

But there may still be areas where we need greater healing and clarity.

:::cue verse | GOD FINISHES HIS WORK | Philippians 1:6 (KJV)
Philippians 1:6 (KJV) Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ:

We need to allow God to continue His work in us.

:::cue section | RESTORATION 01 | Healing Sets New Boundaries
# Healing Sets New Boundaries

:::cue verse | THE WISDOM OF CAUTION | Proverbs 22:3 (KJV)
Proverbs 22:3 (KJV) A prudent man foreseeth the evil, and hideth himself: but the simple pass on, and are punished.

As we heal, we begin understanding what is healthy and what is not.

We recognize that we can forgive someone without continuing to place ourselves in situations where we are repeatedly hurt.

We learn that having boundaries does not mean that we are bitter.

Sometimes it means we have developed wisdom.

:::cue statement | BOUNDARIES ARE NOT BITTERNESS | We can love people without giving them unlimited access.
We can love people without allowing them unlimited access to our lives.

We can forgive people without pretending that their actions have no consequences.

:::cue section | RESTORATION 02 | Healing Defines Relationships
# Healing Defines Relationships

:::cue verse | AS MUCH AS LIES WITHIN YOU | Romans 12:18 (KJV)
Romans 12:18 (KJV) If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men.

Healing helps us understand our relationships more clearly.

We begin to recognize which relationships are healthy, which ones need to change, and which ones require distance.

We stop expecting every person to make up for what someone else failed to give us.

We stop placing expectations on people that have nothing to do with them.

And we begin learning how to love people without constantly operating out of fear, resentment, or insecurity.

:::cue section | RESTORATION 03 | Healing Renews or Replenishes Clarity
# Healing Renews or Replenishes Clarity

:::cue verse | A RENEWED MIND | Ephesians 4:23 (KJV)
Ephesians 4:23 (KJV) And be renewed in the spirit of your mind;

This is something I think is very important.

Remember what we said earlier about hurt muddying the waters?

When we’re hurting, our ability to see situations clearly can become affected.

But healing begins restoring that clarity.

Things that once triggered us don’t necessarily have the same effect.

Situations that once overwhelmed us become easier to navigate.

We begin seeing people for who they actually are instead of constantly seeing them through the experiences of our past.

:::cue statement | WHAT HEALING CHANGES | Healing doesn't erase what happened. It changes how it affects you.
Healing doesn’t necessarily erase what happened to you. It changes the way what happened to you continues to affect you.

And sometimes one of the greatest signs that you’ve healed is that something which once had tremendous control over your emotions no longer controls how you live.

You remember what happened, but you no longer have to keep living in it.
