# How Do We Heal?

So how do we actually begin experiencing this healing?

:::cue steps | THREE PATHS INTO HEALING | God's power
## 1. Through the Power of God

:::cue verse | GOD BINDS WOUNDS | Psalm 147:3 (KJV)
Psalm 147:3 (KJV) He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.

Ultimately, our healing comes from God.

There are things we can work through, things we can understand, and things we can learn about ourselves.

But there is a work that God can do within us that we cannot accomplish through our own strength alone.

We need to allow Him into the places that we’ve been afraid to confront.

We need to let Him deal with the bitterness, resentment, pain, and experiences we’ve carried for so long.

:::cue statement | CHANGE WITHIN | Sometimes we're asking God to change our circumstances when we need Him to heal our hearts.
Sometimes we’re asking God to change our circumstances when what we really need is for Him to heal something within us.

:::cue steps | THREE PATHS INTO HEALING | God's power;Honest conversation
## 2. Through Genuine Processing of Our Emotions and Feelings, Through Conversations

:::cue verse | PRAYER AND HONESTY | James 5:16 (KJV)
James 5:16 (KJV) Confess your faults one to another, and pray one for another, that ye may be healed. The effectual fervent prayer of a righteous man availeth much.

Sometimes we need to talk about what happened.

We need to process what we’re feeling instead of constantly suppressing it.

We need to have honest conversations with people we trust, with people who can pray with us, encourage us, and help us work through what we’re experiencing.

:::cue verse | WISE COUNSEL | Proverbs 11:14 (KJV)
Proverbs 11:14 (KJV) Where no counsel is, the people fall: but in the multitude of counsellors there is safety.

Sometimes that may include receiving wise counsel or professional support.

Not everything is resolved by pretending that we are fine.

Sometimes we have buried things so deeply that we’ve convinced ourselves they no longer affect us.

But healing involves being willing to confront those emotions honestly.

Being able to acknowledge that something hurt you does not make you weak.

Being able to talk about what happened does not mean you have failed spiritually.

And sometimes those conversations are part of how we begin understanding what actually needs to be healed.

:::cue steps | THREE PATHS INTO HEALING | God's power;Honest conversation;Admitting the wound
## 3. Through Actually Recognizing and Admitting That There Is Something Wrong

:::cue verse | SEARCH ME, O GOD | Psalm 139:23 (KJV)
Psalm 139:23 (KJV) Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts:

One of the greatest obstacles to healing is refusing to acknowledge that we are hurt.

Sometimes we’re so accustomed to living a certain way that we don’t even recognize something is wrong.

We think our reactions are normal. We justify our behavior. We blame everyone around us.

But we never stop to ask ourselves, “Why am I reacting this way? Why does this bother me so much? Why do I keep returning to the same things?”

There comes a point where we have to be honest with ourselves and with God.

:::cue verse | TRUTH IN THE INWARD PARTS | Psalm 51:6 (KJV)
Psalm 51:6 (KJV) Behold, thou desirest truth in the inward parts: and in the hidden part thou shalt make me to know wisdom.

We have to admit that there are things inside of us that need attention.

We have to stop pretending that because we are functioning, we must be healed.

And we have to be willing to say, “God, there is something here that I have not dealt with. There is something here that still hurts. There is something in me that needs to change.”

Because we cannot continue ignoring our wounds and expect them to stop affecting our lives.

We have to be willing to let God heal us.

# Invitation: Come to the Healer This Morning

:::cue verse | THE ANOINTING TO HEAL | Isaiah 61:1 (KJV)
Isaiah 61:1 (KJV) The Spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me; because the LORD hath anointed me to preach good tidings unto the meek; he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and the opening of the prison to them that are bound;

This morning, I want to invite you to come to the healer.

Not just with your physical sicknesses. Not just with the things that people can see.

But with the things you’ve been carrying internally.

The hurt that you’ve learned to hide. The resentment that you haven’t been able to let go of. The memories that still bother you. The habits that you continue returning to. The things that happened years ago that still affect how you live today.

Maybe you’ve forgiven the person who hurt you, but you’ve never actually dealt with the pain they caused.

Maybe you’ve been delivered from the situation, but you’re still living with what that situation taught you.

Maybe you’ve spent years convincing yourself that you’re okay, but deep down you know there are still things that need to be healed.

I want you to understand that God is concerned about those things too.

He is concerned about your heart, your thoughts, your emotions, and the things you’ve experienced.

And you don’t have to continue carrying those wounds without bringing them before Him.

:::cue verse | COME UNTO ME | Matthew 11:28 (KJV)
Matthew 11:28 (KJV) Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.

:::cue statement | CLOSING TRUTH | You can be delivered from what hurt you and still need healing from what it taught you.
You can be delivered from what hurt you and still need healing from what it taught you.

So this morning, don’t just ask God to change what is happening around you.

Ask Him to heal what is happening within you.
