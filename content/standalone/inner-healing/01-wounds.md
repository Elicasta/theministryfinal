# The Importance of Inner Healing

We often talk about God healing our bodies and our physical health. We pray for sicknesses, diseases, pain, and physical restoration.

But today, I want to talk to you about the importance of letting God heal your inner self.

Because there are things we experience in life that don’t necessarily leave physical scars, but they leave wounds that can affect us for years.

:::cue statement | CENTRAL TRUTH | You can be delivered from the thing that hurt you and still need healing from what it taught you.

You can be delivered from the thing that hurt you and still need healing from what it taught you.

Because sometimes the wound is no longer there, but the way you learned to see through that wound remains.

You may no longer be in the situation that hurt you. You may no longer be around the people who caused you pain. You may have moved on with your life, but something inside of you has not moved on.

And that’s what I want to talk about today.

:::cue verse | THE HEART OF GOD | Psalm 147:3 (KJV)
Psalm 147:3 (KJV) He healeth the broken in heart, and bindeth up their wounds.

God is not only concerned with healing what is physically broken. He is concerned with healing what is broken within us.

:::cue section | PART ONE | Symptoms of an Unhealed Inner Life
# Symptoms of an Unhealed Inner Life

:::cue points | SYMPTOM 01 | Resentment, a Negative View of Life, and Recurring Cycles
## 1. Resentment, a Negative View of Life, and Recurring Cycles

:::cue verse | THE ROOT OF BITTERNESS | Hebrews 12:15 (KJV)
Hebrews 12:15 (KJV) Looking diligently lest any man fail of the grace of God; lest any root of bitterness springing up trouble you, and thereby many be defiled;

* Many times, we have already gone through the issue, and yet we still resent people and situations. The situation may have ended years ago, but we continue to carry the anger, frustration, and resentment that came from it. We have physically moved past it, but emotionally we are still there.

:::cue verse | EXPECTING THE WORST | Proverbs 28:1 (KJV)
Proverbs 28:1 (KJV) The wicked flee when no man pursueth: but the righteous are bold as a lion.

* Things can be perfectly okay in our lives, and yet we have such a negative view of life. We expect things to go wrong. We expect people to disappoint us. We have a difficult time enjoying what is good because we’re constantly anticipating something bad. Sometimes life isn’t as negative as we think it is. Sometimes we’re seeing life through the lens of what happened to us.

:::cue verse | THE HEART AND PERCEPTION | Proverbs 15:15 (KJV)
Proverbs 15:15 (KJV) All the days of the afflicted are evil: but he that is of a merry heart hath a continual feast.

* We get stuck in the same cycles of life, whether it’s the same patterns, the same circles, or even the same kinds of relationships. We find ourselves repeating the very things that have hurt us. Sometimes we change our environment without ever addressing what is happening inside of us.

:::cue progression | REPEATING THE PATTERN | Wound;Expectation;Reaction;Repeated cycle
And the problem is that until we address what is happening within us, we can continue repeating the same patterns in completely different circumstances.

:::cue points | SYMPTOM 02 | Recurring Thoughts, Sin, and Ultimately Unbreakable Addictions
## 2. Recurring Thoughts, Sin, and Ultimately Unbreakable Addictions

:::cue verse | THE POWER OF HABIT | John 8:34 (KJV)
John 8:34 (KJV) Jesus answered them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin.

* One telltale sign that we have not healed is our inability to break free from the overwhelming amount of recurring thoughts. We constantly revisit conversations, situations, memories, and things that have happened to us. We replay them in our minds, sometimes imagining what we should have said or what we could have done differently. The situation is over, but our minds continue living in it.

:::cue verse | THOUGHTS INTO CAPTIVITY | 2 Corinthians 10:5 (KJV)
2 Corinthians 10:5 (KJV) Casting down imaginations, and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ;

:::cue verse | WHERE WE RUN FOR RELIEF | Romans 6:16 (KJV)
Romans 6:16 (KJV) Know ye not, that to whom ye yield yourselves servants to obey, his servants ye are to whom ye obey; whether of sin unto death, or of obedience unto righteousness?

* Another sign is the inability to break away from sins that give us comfort. Look at your life right now. What do you do when you feel stressed? Where do you run when life gets difficult? Sometimes we develop habitual sins because they temporarily make us feel better. Instead of dealing with what is hurting us, we look for something to distract us from the pain.

* A sign that we have not been healed is our inability to get out of addictions that are damaging us. We know what we’re doing is bad for us. We recognize the consequences. We say, “Just one more time,” or, “This will be the last time.” And yet we keep returning to the same thing.

:::cue verse | NOT BROUGHT UNDER ITS POWER | 1 Corinthians 6:12 (KJV)
1 Corinthians 6:12 (KJV) All things are lawful unto me, but all things are not expedient: all things are lawful for me, but I will not be brought under the power of any.

Sometimes the behavior isn’t just about wanting to do something wrong. Sometimes we’re using the behavior to find relief from something we have never properly dealt with.

That does not excuse sin, but it helps us understand why some things become so difficult to break.

:::cue statement | THE ROOT AND THE FRUIT | We must address both the behavior and what drives us back to it.
We have to deal with the behavior, but we also have to address what keeps driving us back to it.

:::cue section | PART TWO | How to Identify the Wounds
# How to Identify the Wounds

:::cue verse | PRAYER OF EXAMINATION | Psalm 139:23–24 (KJV)
Psalm 139:23–24 (KJV) Search me, O God, and know my heart: try me, and know my thoughts: And see if there be any wicked way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.

In order to be free from these things, we must first identify what is causing the pain.

We have to be willing to look inward and ask ourselves some difficult questions. Some people are truly suffering. When a person tells me that they’re going through depression, or that they cannot seem to break free from a sin, I believe them. I believe that what they’re experiencing is real. Sometimes people are fighting battles internally that we know absolutely nothing about.

:::cue verse | THE PAIN OTHERS CANNOT SEE | Proverbs 14:10 (KJV)
Proverbs 14:10 (KJV) The heart knoweth his own bitterness; and a stranger doth not intermeddle with his joy.

Sometimes it’s a lack of faith, but many times it’s a lack of healing. We are quick to tell people to have more faith, to pray more, or to just get over it. And while faith and prayer are important, sometimes we fail to recognize that a person is genuinely hurting. Not every struggle with depression or unwanted thoughts is a spiritual failure.

Many times, it’s because you have not allowed God to cover your wounds with His healing grace. You have allowed God to change certain things about your life, but there are still places in your heart that you have not allowed Him to touch.

And sometimes that’s because we don’t even recognize how deeply something has affected us.

:::cue statement | FUNCTION IS NOT THE SAME AS HEALING | We have learned to function with the pain instead of actually dealing with it.
We have learned to function with the pain instead of actually dealing with it.

:::cue points | EFFECTS OF UNHEALED PAIN | The Ability of Pain and Hurt to Control Us
# The Ability of Pain and Hurt to Control Us

:::cue verse | THE WOUNDED SPIRIT | Proverbs 18:14 (KJV)
Proverbs 18:14 (KJV) The spirit of a man will sustain his infirmity; but a wounded spirit who can bear?

When we let God heal us, we begin to put an end to the thoughts and habits that have burdened us, because they no longer have the same hold over our lives.

The burden begins to lose its power when the wound that has been feeding it begins to heal.

Think about what happens when you’re physically injured. Even if you’re capable of continuing with your day, the pain changes how you move, how you react, and sometimes even how you treat other people.

The same thing happens internally.

:::cue verse | WHAT SORROW DOES | Proverbs 15:13 (KJV)
Proverbs 15:13 (KJV) A merry heart maketh a cheerful countenance: but by sorrow of the heart the spirit is broken.

:::cue steps | PAIN CHANGES HOW WE | Function;Judge;Perceive
* When you’re hurt, it makes it very difficult to function. You can be physically present somewhere while emotionally being completely somewhere else. Things that should be simple become exhausting because you’re carrying something internally.

:::cue steps | PAIN CHANGES HOW WE | Function;Judge
* When you’re hurt, it makes you biased. You begin judging people and situations through the experience of what happened to you. Someone can have completely innocent intentions, but because of what you’ve experienced before, you assume the worst.

:::cue steps | PAIN CHANGES HOW WE | Function;Judge;Perceive
* When you’re hurt, it muddies the waters. Your judgment becomes clouded. You have a difficult time separating what is actually happening from what you are feeling. Sometimes you aren’t responding to the present situation. You’re responding to a previous wound that the present situation reminded you of.

:::cue points | THE CONSEQUENCES | Hurt Will Make You:
# Hurt Will Make You:

:::cue verse | ANGER AND RESTRAINT | Proverbs 19:11 (KJV)
Proverbs 19:11 (KJV) The discretion of a man deferreth his anger; and it is his glory to pass over a transgression.

:::cue steps | HURT WILL MAKE YOU | Hold on to offenses
* Hold on to things longer than necessary. You continue carrying conversations, offenses, and situations that should have been left behind a long time ago. Sometimes the other person has moved on, but you’re still emotionally attached to what happened.

:::cue steps | HURT WILL MAKE YOU | Hold on to offenses;Exaggerate situations
* Exaggerate situations. Something small happens, but because it touches an old wound, your response becomes much greater than the situation itself. It isn’t necessarily just about what happened today. It’s about everything that happened before.

:::cue steps | HURT WILL MAKE YOU | Hold on to offenses;Exaggerate situations;Cut unrelated people off
* Cut people off who are completely unrelated to your current situation. You begin treating people as though they are responsible for things they never did. You punish people in your present because of what someone else did in your past.

:::cue steps | HURT WILL MAKE YOU | Hold on to offenses;Exaggerate situations;Cut unrelated people off;Shift blame
* Remain unaccountable for your behavior and shift the blame to everyone and everything else. Because you’ve been hurt, you begin justifying your reactions. And instead of recognizing when you’re wrong, you blame your circumstances, your past, and the people around you.

And we have to understand that while the pain we experienced may not have been our fault, that does not mean we are no longer responsible for how we treat other people.

:::cue verse | PERSONAL RESPONSIBILITY | Galatians 6:5 (KJV)
Galatians 6:5 (KJV) For every man shall bear his own burden.

We cannot allow what someone did to us to become the reason we continue hurting ourselves and others.
